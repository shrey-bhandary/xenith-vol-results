# Xenith selected: the personal line

One line per person, pulled from their form answers. It is the only sentence on
the page that is about them, so it does the work of being chosen. Everything
here is sized for a static page opened from a private link.

## The contract

- 55 to 120 characters. One or two sentences. A third only if it is four words
  or less.
- Sentence case, a period at the end. No exclamation marks, no em dashes.
- One joke, maximum, and it lands on Xenith.
- It contains a noun the person chose: a time, a place, an object, a tool, a
  title, a food, a name.
- No compliment about character. "You are passionate" is out. "The mic is
  yours" is in.
- It reads like a friend replying at midnight after reading the form, not like
  a certificate.

## Fields to lines

| Form field | Code | What it really tells us | Line family |
|---|---|---|---|
| What are you bad at, or want to get better at? | WEAK | What they are scared of, and what they will forgive us for | Placement |
| What have you organised or made before? | PAST | What they have already survived | Record |
| What is your favourite thing you have made? | WORK | What they are proud of when nobody is watching | Craft |
| If you could run one event at Xenith, what would it be? | IDEA | What they want to see happen | Pitch |
| Anything else we should know? | FREE | The detail they added at 1 AM | Odd detail |
| Why Xenith? | WHY | Motive. Rarely has a hook | Motive |
| Department preference | DEPT | The job, not the person | Fallback only |

**WEAK becomes placement.** Three modes. Carve-out keeps the thing away from them
("You said evening classes are your nemesis. We put the chaos at 12 PM."). Cure
hands them the thing on purpose because the role needs it ("You edit everything
three times. The sponsor deck has been waiting its whole life for you.").
Contribution invites a harmless trait in as an asset ("You said you overthink
colour. Xenith has one accent and no regrets. Come argue."). Never diagnose,
never advise, never use a WEAK answer that would embarrass them if
screenshotted.

**PAST becomes record.** Name the thing they ran, then one true small detail
about that kind of work. "You've set up three school fests. You already know
where the extension cords live." The part after their record is ours to add: an
inference about the work or a promise about the role, never a new fact about
them.

**WORK becomes craft.** Put their object on a Xenith surface. "You made a comic
about a pigeon running for mayor. Xenith needs that pigeon on a poster." This
family says we looked, not that we are impressed.

**IDEA becomes pitch.** Say yes with a constraint, a room, or a budget. "You
want an event where bad startup ideas get funded. Xenith has fake money and a
real stage." Never confirm an event that has not been approved. A conversation,
a slot in the ideas meeting, or a room we already have are safe to promise.

**FREE becomes odd detail.** The best material is often here. Use the detail and
skip the sentiment.

**WHY becomes motive.** Usually abstract ("I love tech"). Use it only if it has a
place, a person, or a specific memory. Otherwise drop to the next field.

**DEPT is a fallback, not a family.** It names labour, never character. See
below.

## Which field wins

Take the first field with a usable hook. Stop.

1. WEAK, if it has a concrete admission and passes the safety list. Most
   personal, and the easiest to make funny without flattery.
2. WORK, if there is a named object.
3. PAST, if there is a named event, role, or team.
4. IDEA, if the event can be named in four words.
5. FREE, if the detail is strange in a good way.
6. WHY, if it has a place or a person.
7. The department line.
8. The default line ("You are in. Rusty has been told to behave.").

Tiers within a field:

- A: the answer contains a noun the person chose that we can put next to a
  Xenith noun. Use it near verbatim.
- B: the answer is a stance or feeling. Compress it to a clause, then answer it
  with a real thing.
- C: generic, under six words, or a compliment about themselves ("hardworking",
  "teamwork", "I love tech"). Not usable. Go to the next field.

Two clauses from two fields is allowed, but the second clause must be Xenith's,
not a second thing about them. Never three.

## Five tone rules

1. Quote them, do not describe them. The line must contain the noun they chose.
   Test: swap in another selected person's name. If it still reads true, delete
   it and write the real one.
2. Aim the joke at Xenith. The funny clause is our chaos, our missing docs, our
   one accent colour, our fake money. Tease the fest, never the applicant. No
   jokes about marks, rank, attendance, body, money, or family.
3. The second move must be checkable. A room, a slot, a piece of equipment, a
   schedule, a person, or a precise thing we noticed. "You will be great" is not
   checkable. "The mic is yours" is.
4. Weakness becomes placement, not a diagnosis. They told us something they are
   bad at. Use it to put them somewhere it helps, or somewhere it cannot hurt.
   Never "you should", never therapy language, never a compliment that starts
   with "despite".
5. One breath, one joke, one period. Sentence case, 55 to 120 characters, one
   or two sentences, third only if four words or less. No exclamation, no em dash, no
   "thrilled", "journey", "passion", "excited", "proud", "rockstar", "family".
   Dry, warm, and specific enough that it could only have been written after
   reading the form.

## Fallbacks

**Thin answer (Tier C).** Use the department line. It names the actual labour, so
it is still about their role. Never a generic compliment.

| Department | Fallback line |
|---|---|
| Events & Admin | Events is 40 minutes of stage and six weeks of chairs. You're on chairs. |
| Tech | The projector has one job and a history of failure. You're the plan B. |
| Design | Every poster gets approved three times. You're the third time. |
| Marketing | Social is three posts a day and a group chat that never sleeps. You bring the snacks. |
| Sponsorship | Sponsorship is eleven emails for one yes. You write the twelfth. |
| Content | Somebody has to write the announcements nobody reads. You'll make them read it. |

**Missing field.** Next field in the ladder. Do not invent an answer and do not
staple two half lines together.

**Sensitive answer.** Health, mental health, family money, marks or rank, caste,
religion, relationships, disability. Do not quote it and do not allude to it,
even kindly. Drop to the next field. If it is the only field, use the department
line. Self-insults ("I'm useless", "I'm the dumb one") are also never quoted.
This page gets screenshotted.

**Contradictory or vague.** Trust the more specific field. If neither has a noun,
department line.

**Hinglish or Marathi.** One phrase can stay in their language inside an English
line, with no translation and no italics. Skip it if the phrase needs a
footnote.

**No record at all.** The existing default line. It makes a joke and claims
nothing.

The reveal pauses on the first sentence period, which is the turn in every

## Banned

Words: thrilled, excited, exciting, proud, honoured, journey, passion,
passionate, amazing, incredible, rockstar, superstar, family, blessed, grateful,
"we can't wait".

Moves: exclamation marks, em dashes, all caps, "not just X but Y", rhetorical
questions, two compliments in a row, quoting a mark or rank, promising a role or
a certificate, dates that could move.

`node triage.js` checks length, sentence count, the four-word tail rule, banned
words, punctuation, and that every record has a department fallback.

## Before freeze

- Screenshot test: would they send this to their mother, their crush, and their
  group chat? If one of the three would sting, rewrite.
- Fact check: every schedule, room, number, and event name against the run
  sheet. Katherine's 12 PM must be true in the final schedule or the line
  changes.
- Name check: preferred name, spelling, department label. The line is personal,
  and the label has to be right too.
- Privacy: `site/data.js` ships every line to every visitor. If the lines carry
  weakness answers, anyone with one link can read all twelve. Ship per-person
  pages with the record inlined, or split the data file per slug.

## At scale

1. Export the form to CSV.
2. Sort by answer length and flag Tier C by eye. No script can pick the funny
   noun.
3. Write the first twenty by hand. Do not template.
4. Write in batches of ten, then read the batch in one sitting. Lines written
   together start to rhyme, so break the rhythm.
5. Freeze into `lines.js`, run the lint, then generate pages. No runtime
   generation: the page must never compose a sentence a person has not read.
